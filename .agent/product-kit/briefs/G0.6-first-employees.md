# G0.6 brief: first AI employees, their order, and autonomy ceilings

Status: DRAFT for the founder's decision, prepared 2026-10-20. Labels: Verified (repo or primary source), Reported (secondary), Inference, Unknown.

## Decision needed
Which AI employees to hire first and in what order, with a maximum autonomy level for each (levels in `VISION.md` section 6). Each hire still needs its own approval: `PRODUCT.md` allows no additional employees without demonstrated need and founder approval.

## What is true today
- **Verified:** four employees exist in code: Sophia Vance (COO in the repo; you now call Sofia the CEO, see G0.4), the Researcher, the PM and the Finance employee. There is no Engineer, Marketer, Support or Ops role, and no per-employee memory.
- **Verified:** the roster is bounded by `PRODUCT.md`, and uncoordinated swarms are ruled out.

## Evidence
- **Reported:** an orchestrator with parallel workers beat a single agent by 90.2% on Anthropic's research eval, at roughly 15 times a chat's tokens. Anthropic says shared-context work such as coding fits poorly.
- **Reported:** Paperclip models agents with roles, reporting lines, permissions and monthly budgets. OpenHands (MIT) is a sandboxed coding-agent platform, a candidate engine for an Engineer.
- **Reported:** Gartner forecast that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by 2027, citing cost, unclear value and weak risk controls.

## Options
- **A. Product order:** Engineer, then Support, then Marketer, then Ops.
- **B. Customer-facing first:** Support, then Marketer, Engineer later.
- **C. No new hires yet:** give the existing four tools and skills, and hire only when an evaluation shows a gap.

## Recommended default (Inference)
C first, then A, one role at a time. A role is hired only when: the pilot product is chosen (G15.1); the tools it needs exist behind the gateway (G14); and a single agent with skills failed that role's evaluation baseline (G13.6).

Proposed ceilings: Engineer L2 on a branch, production deploy founder-only. Support L1 (drafts), L2 after graduation. Marketer L1 then L2, publishing always with approval. Ops L2 with read-only access plus proposals. The existing four keep their current ceilings.

## The attack
- Role-named agents can look like a company without producing value. Each employee costs tokens, an evaluation effort, a memory to maintain and a permission surface.
- If one agent with the right skills passes the role's evaluation, do not hire. That is the single-agent argument, and it is testable here.
- Customer-facing roles carry legal and brand risk, so they come last.

## What would change this
Evaluation results; the type of pilot product; your budget.
